Small-reactor start-ups have raised $4.5bn to power AI data centers. Here is who is leading the race.

| | | | Rift Capital | RIFT CAPITAL | | --- | --- | | Market Insight  ·  No. 6 | | --- | --- | --- | --- |  |  | | --- | | | NUCLEAR ENERGY AI needs power. Nuclear start-ups want to supply it. AI data centers need electricity around the clock. Small-reactor start-ups promise to supply it, and investors are already paying for that promise.  Paul Ravignon  ·  October 2026  ·  3 min read | | BloombergNEF expects 194 GW of US data-center capacity by 2035, close to a fifth of the country’s electricity. In January, Meta signed nuclear deals with Vistra, TerraPower and Oklo supporting up to 6.6 GW.  Conventional plants take years and billions to build. That leaves room for companies that want to make reactors like products: small, standardised, built in a factory and shipped to the customer. | | | 194 GW  US data-center capacity expected by 2035 | $4.5bn  into nuclear start-ups by July 2026 | ~$6bn  Valar’s reported valuation, unconfirmed | | --- | --- | --- | | | BUILDING REACTORS LIKE PRODUCTS  Conventional nuclear plants are enormous projects, often designed around a specific site and assembled over many years. Small modular reactors (SMRs) are designed to use more factory-made components, while microreactors take the idea further, with units small enough to be transported and installed closer to where electricity is needed.  Manufacturing reactors repeatedly using the same design could reduce construction time and costs as production grows. Aalo CEO Matt Loszak said in February 2025 that 95% of the company’s system would be factory-built, while CTO Yasir Arafat described plans in March 2026 for a factory capable of producing at least 100 reactors a year at full capacity. These manufacturing targets remain untested at commercial scale.  Valar and Aalo are pursuing this approach with different reactor designs, both aimed at supplying electricity to AI data centers. | | |  | Valar Atomics | Aalo Atomics | | --- | --- | --- | | COOLING | Helium, graphite-moderated | Sodium, liquid metal | | FUEL | TRISO particles | Uranium enriched to ~5%, Urenco contract | | CRITICAL | June 18, 2026, Utah | July 4, 2026, Idaho National Laboratory | | AI PARTNER | Nvidia, 30 MW data center explored in Utah | Crusoe, modular data center demo in 2027 | | NEXT STEP | Project Beehive, about 456 small reactors | 50 MWe plants at Crusoe sites by end of 2029 | | | VALAR ATOMICS  Valar is developing a graphite-moderated, helium-cooled reactor fueled with TRISO particles, a design the company says needs no water to cool the reactor itself. On July 1, 2026, Valar and Nvidia announced they would jointly explore a 30 MW AI data center in Utah, powered by Valar’s reactor and designed to use almost no water. Valar has also proposed Project Beehive, a nuclear-powered data center campus on federal land near Price, Utah; according to a company proposal reviewed by NPR, it would include about 456 small reactors. The Bureau of Land Management is reviewing the application. | | AALO ATOMICS  Aalo is developing a sodium-cooled reactor that uses liquid metal to carry heat away from the core. In July 2026, it announced a partnership with Crusoe, an AI infrastructure company, to demonstrate nuclear-powered computing at Idaho National Laboratory. The companies plan to power a modular data center in 2027 before pursuing larger commercial deployments, giving Aalo a prospective user for its technology, although electricity delivery to an operating data center remains unproven. | | BEYOND AI  Antares is developing microreactors for military applications, while France’s Jimmy and Finland’s Steady Energy are building reactors to supply industrial heat and district heating. Larger developers such as TerraPower and X-energy are working on plants that can supply electricity to the grid and industrial customers. | | THE FUEL QUESTION  Many advanced reactors require high-assay low-enriched uranium, known as HALEU, which contains a higher concentration of the uranium isotope used in nuclear fission than the fuel typically used in conventional plants. Commercial supplies remain limited in the US, where the government is funding new production capacity.  Aalo’s reactor, however, is designed to use uranium enriched to about 5%, within the range commonly used by today’s nuclear plants. In September 2025, Aalo announced a commercial contract with Urenco, a major uranium enrichment provider, for the delivery of low-enriched uranium for its Aalo-X reactor, giving it access to a more established enrichment market, although it still needs to manufacture and qualify the finished reactor fuel. | | [How four reactors reached criticality →](https://rift-capital.com/en/blog/nuclear-startups-ai-electricity-demand) | | THE MONEY   | | Valar Atomics  Helium-cooled reactor, Nvidia AI site planned · Sequoia | $1bn | | --- | --- | | | --- | --- | --- | | | Antares  Microreactors for defense and space · Paradigm, Caffeinated | $470m | | --- | --- | | | | Proxima Fusion  Stellarator fusion plants · XTX, East X · RWE, Google | €411m | | --- | --- | | | | Atlas Atomics  Design not public · General Catalyst, a16z (reported) | ~$400m | | --- | --- | | | | Blue Energy  Grid-scale reactors built in shipyards · VXI Capital | $380m | | --- | --- | | | | Radiant  Kaleidos microreactor · Draper, Boost VC · Lockheed Martin | $300m+ | | --- | --- | | | | Aalo Atomics  Sodium-cooled reactor, Crusoe demo in 2027 · Valor · NRG, Hitachi | $100m | | --- | --- | | | | Last Energy  PWR-5 reactor · Astera Institute | $100m+ | | --- | --- | |   Latest round shown, with its lead investors.
