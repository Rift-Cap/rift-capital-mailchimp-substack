Europe’s defense start-ups build at home. Their biggest cheques come from America, and their strategic seats go to Airbus, Dassault and Saab.

A new wave of European defense start-ups is raising record rounds. Their engineers and factories are European. Their largest cheques often are not.  Paul Ravignon  ·  September 2026  ·  3 min read | | In May 2025, Alta Ares raised €2 million. In June 2026, it raised €50 million, all of it from European funds. The Paris company builds small drones that hunt other drones, and its systems are already in use in conflict zones.  Three things pushed Europe here. Governments decided to rearm and buy European. Ukraine gave the industry a place to test products in weeks, not years. And the maths changed: a Russian Shahed drone costs about $35,000, while the missile fired at it can cost millions. | | | $8.7bn  raised by European defense start-ups in 2025 | 3×  growth in late-stage rounds, to $4.7bn | €150bn  in EU loans for European-made defense | | --- | --- | --- | | | WHO SITS ON THE CAP TABLES  At €50 million, European funds can lead a round. Green marks a European incumbent as shareholder or partner. | | WHAT THE RULE MISSES  The American stakes are minority stakes, and for now that is fine. But investors need an exit one day, and the deepest markets for defense listings are in the US. Over time, money can move where decisions get made.  The incumbents may matter more. Airbus co-led Quantum Systems’ round, Dassault led Harmattan AI’s, and Rheinmetall now runs joint ventures with ICEYE and Destinus. A start-up with a big group on its cap table sells more easily into its programmes, and less easily against them.  Europe’s SAFE programme checks where components are made. It does not check who owns the company. | | THE NEXT TEST  In October 2025, Stark’s Virtus missed its target four times out of four. Weeks later, Germany’s chief of defence reported a hit rate above 90%. That is the speed investors are paying for.  The next test is the factory. A drone that works in trials has to work at the ten-thousandth unit, at a price that still makes sense against a Shahed. | | | Europe will have its drones, built on its soil under its rules.
